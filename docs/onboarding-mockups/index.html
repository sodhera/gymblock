How the haptics and sounds are scored

Strength follows the story

How strong a haptic is tracks what the scene is about. Focus fading means each pulse gets weaker. Steady focus means pulses stay level. A pump that builds gets stronger with every set.

Sharpness matches the material

A lock clicks: short and crisp. A kettlebell lands: heavy and dull. Notifications use the iOS double tap. The signature is a light, grainy scratch.

Silence carries meaning

In the scrolling pump scene, the long rests get no haptic at all, only faint scroll ticks. The gap is the point.

Sound sits under the haptic

Each haptic has a quiet matching sound: rising notes for building, falling notes for forgetting, one chime when you arrive. The sound stays quiet, follows the silent switch and can be turned off.

Tap: height is strength, darker is sharper Continuous: rising or falling strength Sound

To build this in the app: Core Haptics patterns and short sounds, one per scene. Today the app plays one-off taps through UIKit and has two sound files, which are off by default. A web page can't make your iPhone vibrate, so here the haptic is drawn as a track and the sound plays. The real feel has to be checked on a device, not the simulator.

What changes, and why

  1. The first screen shows the change, not the mechanism.Today it shows notifications folding under a lock. The new opening pairs what you do (stop scrolling, record everything) with what you get (stay focused, get jacked) and shows the arm going from grey to green.
  2. Someone is talking.Pages read like a form today. Sulav's note sets the tone; after it the app speaks as "we", plainly, with no pretending.
  3. Three questions go nowhere.Gender and height are saved and only shown in Profile. Weight only picks kg or lb, which moves to the plan page.
  4. The yearly number uses your real week.Today it assumes 5 workouts a week for everyone. The new days-a-week question feeds the hours-a-year page, the weekly goal on Home and the split suggestion.
  5. Haptics tell the story.Today: one-off taps. Proposed: patterns that fade with your focus, build with the pump and land with the kettlebell.
  6. The paywall sells the change.Today it lists three features. Now it shows your two paths, Now vs With GymBlock, in your own numbers, plus a Today / Day 5 / Day 7 trial timeline. Unrot uses a two-paths screen, Opal shows bad news then good news, and ClearSpace has a before/after graphic.
  7. Splits after paying, as one tap.Nobody is asked about splits before they've decided to buy. After paying, one question picks a template, and the flow ends one tap from your first locked set.

Decisions for you

  1. Paywall headline.Three options under P20; tap one to see it on the screen. All three sell the change without predicting a body result. The bars say "34 min of timed rest", not "46 min training", because rests stay rests.
  2. Blocking page title.Three options under P16. My pick is "Which apps should we lock while you train?"
  3. Notification sounds.Instagram's and TikTok's own tones belong to Meta and ByteDance, so we can't put them in the app, and these mockups can't either. Most apps use the iPhone's default alert anyway. In the app, these moments can play iOS's real system alert through Apple's sound API. Here each app gets an original glass ding of its own.
  4. "Get jacked" on the welcome screen.Your line, and it lands. Note that it promises a body outcome, which the project rules otherwise avoid. Say if you'd rather soften it, for example to "Train harder".
  5. Sound on by default in onboarding?Today sound is off by default. I'd play it in onboarding and keep it under the silent switch, so a phone on silent stays quiet.
  6. Sulav's real signature and photo.The mockup uses a handwriting font. A scan of his real signature, and maybe a small photo, would make the note feel real.

How this was made. Current screens are real captures from the Debug build on the iPhone 18 Pro Max simulator (iOS 27). The current paywall is the 15 Oct capture with test-store prices, and the current splits page shows three splits already added. Proposed screens are HTML mockups in the app's type, palette and sizes, using the approved brain and arm art cut from the app. The app itself is unchanged.

The welcome and mind-muscle scenes are drawn frame by frame: the arm is the app's own artwork (OnboardingArm) bent at the elbow with the same mesh formula as ArmIllustration.swift, and the brain uses the paths from BenefitScenes.swift. Sounds are synthesized in the page.

Numbers on the proposed screens use the sample answers: 2-minute rests and 4 workouts a week. 34 phone minutes × 4 × 52 ÷ 60 = 118 hours a year, which is 157 workouts of 45 minutes. The founder's note is Sulav's text, with the list items capitalized.
