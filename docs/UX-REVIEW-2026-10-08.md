# GymBlock UI/UX review — 8 Oct 2026

> **Status, later the same day: implemented.** Every P0 and every P1 item below was built in the redesign pass recorded in [DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md) (brand, stage, surfaces, type, illustration, voice) and proven in [screenshots/redesign-2026-10-08](../screenshots/redesign-2026-10-08/). Results and test runs are in [VALIDATION.md](../VALIDATION.md). The text below is the review as written before the work, kept for the before/after record.

Walked as a brand-new user on the **iPhone 18 Pro / iOS 27.0** simulator ("GymBlock Review"), Debug build at commit `15ec524`: fresh install → all 19 onboarding pages → empty Home → a free workout (first-time exercise, set, rest, rest's up, pause, end, summary) → then `--demo` data for Home, History, progress, a split workout, the Lock Screen Live Activity, and an accessibility text size. Every claim below was seen on screen; the captures are in [screenshots/review-2026-10-08](../screenshots/review-2026-10-08/).

The brief for this pass: the app should feel *really good, polished, carefully crafted, with pleasant visuals*. This review is written against that bar, not against "does it work".

## Verdict

The structure is right and the workout loop is good. What makes it feel cheap is everything *around* the structure:

1. **It has no face.** The app icon is a solid black square. There is no wordmark, no glyph, no colour identity. The first thing a person sees on the Home Screen says "unfinished".
2. **It launches with a flash.** A white cover fades out over a dark launch screen, so the first frame of the welcome page is washed to light grey for about a third of a second. Measured from a screen recording (frame brightness 0 → 114 → 0 on a 0–255 scale).
3. **Everything is the same grey.** Background, glass cards, ring disc, tiles, steppers and sheets all sit within a few percent of the same near-black. The "ember" glow and dots are barely visible, so Liquid Glass has nothing to refract and reads as flat rounded rectangles. There is no depth, no layering, no warmth.
4. **Half of every page is empty.** The fixed grid pins a 28-pt headline to the top and the button to the bottom, and most stages (name, gender, height, weight, minutes, commit, offer, Home) fill 30–40 % of the space between. Content floats in a void instead of owning the page.
5. **The illustrations don't match the rest.** The brain with a face, the raster arm, and SF Symbols standing in for Instagram/TikTok/X look like placeholders beside the austere type and the charts. The abstract visuals (phone-time ring, dot grid, the two line charts) are the strongest pages in the app; the figurative ones are the weakest.
6. **The voice is clinical.** "Illustration", "Example", "Press and hold.", "Blocking is simulated in this build.", "Subscriptions are not available in this review build.", "Skip paywall (Debug build)". Honest qualifiers are required; these are written like release notes, and the last two are the final impression of onboarding.
7. **Onboarding makes you wait.** Ten pages hide Continue until a 2.5–5 s scene finishes, roughly 35 s of forced waiting with no tap-to-skip. By the fourth story page it feels like a chore, and three of the nineteen pages (gender, height, weight) collect data the app doesn't use yet.

Plus six real defects found on the walk (next section).

## P1 — the visual system

These are the changes that move the app from "wireframe in dark mode" to "crafted".

**Stage.** Make the ember real. A larger, warmer radial glow (two layers: a wide 6–8 % wash and a tighter 12–14 % core behind the stage area), dots at 1.2 px with a visible falloff, and a very soft vignette at the edges. Then glass has something to bend and the page stops being flat. Keep "never a red background": this is warmth, not red.

**Surfaces.** Introduce three tones instead of one: stage (0.035), raised glass (~0.09–0.11 with a 1-px top highlight at 8 % white), and the one bright surface (the white button). Today cards, the ring disc and the steppers are visually the same as the background behind them (`26-workout-ready-first.png`, `35b-summary.png`, `41-history.png`).

**Type.** The headline is `.title` bold (28 pt). For one-idea-per-page it should be `.largeTitle` bold (34 pt) with tight tracking, and single-line headlines should be centred in the reserved two-line box rather than top-aligned above a hole. Secondary text at 0.64 white is fine; tertiary at 0.5 is too faint on the captions that matter ("Stays on this phone.").

**Red.** Pick one rule and keep it. The spec says red is the ≤ 2 % signal, yet a selected answer becomes a large red-tinted block (`03b-gender-selected.png`, `21-chooser.png`) while the primary action stays white. Either selection = white glass + red check only, or accept the tint and use it consistently (including the week card's today dot, the live set dot, and the gain numbers). Right now it reads as two different design decisions.

**Controls.** The back button is a heavy 44-pt glass disc beside a 3-pt progress line; the pair is unbalanced. The pause pill ("⏸ 0:16") looks like a clock, not a control. The unit switch under the wheels is the plain system segmented control. The split editor, set editor and Training answers are unstyled system Forms inside glass sheets. None of these are wrong; together they are what makes the app feel assembled rather than designed.

**Illustrations.** Replace, not refine: the brain-with-a-face (`10b-mindA-end.png`), the raster arm (`11b`–`13b`), and the symbol app tiles (`16-blocking.png`). Two options that fit the app's strengths: (a) stay abstract everywhere: signal lines, pulses, bars and dots, in the same language as the reveal ring and the charts; (b) commission one consistent set of thin-line figures. (a) is cheaper and already half-built.

**Voice.** Keep every qualifier, rewrite them as a person would say them: "Illustration" → drop (the page is obviously one) or a small "Illustrative" at the axis; "Press and hold." → put it inside the button ("Hold to commit" already says it); "Blocking is simulated in this build." → "Blocking is a preview for now."; "Subscriptions are not available in this review build." → "Subscriptions aren't on yet in this build."; "Skip paywall (Debug build)" → "Continue without subscribing · Debug"; "Delete account" → "Erase everything" (there is no account; the footer admits it).

### Home

`20-home-empty.png`, `36-home-one.png`, `40-home-demo.png`

Works: the one-decision layout, the week card (today ring, "2 of 5", last workout), rotating splits, Start in the fixed place.

Doesn't: the page is ~60 % empty between the headline block and the card. There is no warmth at all: no greeting, no time of day, no streak, no "last time you added 5 kg". For a motivation app the first screen after onboarding should make the person want to tap Start; today it's a label and a button. The History and Settings discs are generic. The chooser sheet (`21-chooser.png`) shows the red-block selection problem at full size.

### Workout

`25`–`34`, `51`–`54`

This is the best screen and should set the standard for the rest. Fixed grid, ring, two steppers, honest caption, one button. The rest's-up state (`32-rest-up.png`) is the single strongest visual in the app. The keyboard behaviour (ring folds away, button stays above the keys, `54-keyboard.png`) is well done.

To fix: ring disc and track are nearly invisible in the ready state, so it reads as a grey circle with text (`26`, `51`); set dots at 9 pt are too small to read as progress; the pause control looks like a read-only clock; the End dialog (P0 #5); accessibility-size clipping (P0 #6). The Sets sheet (`30`) and set editor (`31`) are bare system lists with a glass frame.

### Summary

`35b-summary.png`, `52-stage-summary.png`

Four identical grey tiles, a recap card, then a large empty gap, then Done. The count-up is nice but there is no celebration, no hierarchy (Time and Weight moved look the same), no first-workout variant, and "Better than last time" only appears when a gain exists, so the common case is four numbers. For a one-set workout "220 kg moved" is honest but odd as the headline stat.

### History and progress

`41-history.png`, `42`–`47`

History reads like a settings screen: two grey tiles, a row, a list. There is no chart on the hub, no PRs, no streak; the exercise names in rows truncate with "…". Exercise progress lands on an empty scope (P0 #4). The Before/After page (`44-progress-bench.png`) is a good idea rendered as left-aligned text with a hairline; the chart is one more tap away (`45-all-records.png`). The workout detail (`47`) is a plain table, which is fine for a detail page.

### Settings

`24-settings.png`, `24b-settings-bottom.png`

A correct system Form on the stage; toggles tinted red read well. "Delete account" with the footer "Your account lives only on this iPhone" contradicts itself; rename until a login exists.

### Lock Screen

`55-lockscreen.png`

The Live Activity layout is clear and the Start set button is where the thumb is. No changes beyond matching whatever tones the app adopts.

## Recommended order of work

1. **Fix the six P0s** (icon, launch flash, gated buttons, progress scope, End dialog, large-text clipping). One session.
2. **Visual system pass**: stage glow and dots, three surface tones, headline size and centring, one red rule, control balance. This single pass changes how every screen feels and needs no new screens. Prove it with the same captures.
3. **Replace the figurative art** with the abstract language already used by the reveal ring and the charts. Drop the arm and the brain.
4. **Trim onboarding**: merge gender/height/weight into one "About you" page or defer them; let Continue appear after ~1 s on scene pages (the scene keeps playing); rewrite the captions.
5. **Warm up Home and Summary**: a greeting line, the last like-for-like gain on Home, a first-workout and a PR variant on Summary, "Better than last time" first when it exists.
6. **History hub**: a small weekly bar chart in place of two tiles, PRs, no truncation.

Items 1–2 are the ones that change the first impression. Nothing above touches the data model, blocking semantics, billing, or the honesty rules in AGENTS.md.
