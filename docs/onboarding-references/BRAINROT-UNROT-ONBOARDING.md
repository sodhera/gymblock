## The structure they share

Both follow one arc. Neither is a questionnaire with a price at the end; the questions are inside a story.

1. **Hook with proof.** Ratings and user counts on the first screen (Unrot), real screenshots of the product (Brainrot).
2. **Meet the problem in your own life.** The person is made to say it: Unrot's chips ("yea am i cooked?", "ouchie"), Brainrot's slider and the questions about feelings, goals, when they lose control.
3. **The cost, at a bigger scale each page.** Hours a day → month → days a year (Unrot); a year → 22 years of a life (Brainrot). Then the dot grid, which both use: a life as dots, the lost part red.
4. **Absolution, then an enemy.** "It's not your fault" / "You're not addicted, your brain is being exploited." Thousands of engineers, PhDs, billions. Shame makes people close the app; an enemy makes the app an ally.
5. **Why what you tried fails.** Screen Time limits get ignored, blockers get deleted, willpower "lol". This is the objection handler for *iOS already has Screen Time, why pay?*
6. **The product, shown.** Unrot's three-step demo; Brainrot's lock screen and chat on the paywall's first page.
7. **The promise, as a picture.** "7 more years of Learning / Playing / Growing"; "you won't feel guilt, because you've earned it".
8. **Credibility stacked right before the price.** Testimonials, counts, university crests, "made for people like you".
9. **Permissions after investment, each with a reason.** Notifications as "gentle reminders"; tracking as "help the brain learn".
10. **A commitment ritual.** Hold a button and watch the screen fill. Both do it, both right before the "plan is ready" page.
11. **"Your personalized plan is ready."** A fake loader (Analyzing your answers…), then a bridge page with the person's name.
12. **A trial paywall.** "No payment due now", a timeline of the trial with a promised reminder, the yearly plan preselected.

## Why it works

- **The brain is the person.** Both apps personify the user's own brain, give it moods, and let the person hurt it (Brainrot's slider) and heal it (the stones stacking as you hold). Abstract screen time becomes a creature you feel for, and the creature is the app icon, so the feeling carries into the app and the Home screen.
- **Every tap is a yes.** Unrot's chips are all equivalent, but the person taps "i want to join them", "show me how!", "Great", "Got it!", "Let's go" before they ever see a price. Brainrot gets the same from "Good to know", "Makes sense", "Let's do this!". By the paywall they have agreed thirty times.
- **Length is the point.** Four minutes and forty taps is sunk cost and time to build the case. Both apps spend more than half their onboarding before the first real question.
- **Their own number, then their own life.** The scrolling hours come from the person; the arithmetic is shown ("5 hours a day → 150 hours a month → 76 days a year"); then the number is put against a lifetime, which they can't argue with because they supplied the input.
- **The pivot.** After the worst number comes "the good news": years back, and what those years are (learning, playing, growing). The cost page is never the last thing before the ask.
- **Shame is removed before the sale.** "It's not your fault", "we don't judge", "no shame, no punishment". The person is a victim of engineers, not weak.
- **Alternatives are closed off.** Both name every free fix (Screen Time, blockers, deleting apps, willpower) and say why it failed, so the price has no free competitor left.
- **The paywall sells the trial, not the plan.** "Try for $0.00", "No payment due now", a Day 2 reminder promised in writing. The reminder promise is what makes a 3-day trial feel safe to start.

## What Brainrot does that Unrot doesn't

The onboarding alone can't explain a 10x gap. The recordings show distribution and pricing differences too, and those probably matter more. But in the flow itself:

- **The person does the damage.** The slider that melts the brain is the single most memorable moment in either app. Unrot's chat is 75 seconds of reading grey bubbles with fake choices; its demo is passive.
- **A lifetime, not a year.** Unrot stops at 76 days a year. Brainrot asks your age, then shows 22 years of your life, your life as dots with "you are here", and "7 years back". Bigger scale, and the pivot is concrete (learning, playing, growing, relaxing, living), not a line graph.
- **A named result.** "Your attention profile is The Compulsive Checker" with two bars. It is a quiz result: identity, screenshot-able, and Sulav lingered on it longest. Unrot has nothing like it.
- **The case is denser before the price.** Willpower → why Brainrot works → research → made for people like you → this week it will help you, in the last 30 seconds. Unrot's last screens are a definition of a verb and a fake loader.
- **The paywall is a wall with a trial.** Three pages: the product, the reminder promise, the plans. "No payment due now" on all three. Trial only on yearly, so yearly is the obvious pick. Unrot's paywall has a close button from the first frame, no trial, and immediate billing; it leaks.
- **Warmth and a wider audience.** Brainrot: "we don't judge", "no shame". Unrot: "you're not special", "willpower → lol", coins for photographing your made bed. Brainrot's "Which best describes you?" lists Builder, Knowledge worker, Founder, Athlete: adults who pay. Brainrot also charges more (AED 229.99 against 199.99 a year; a 34.99 monthly against a 29.99 weekly).
- **Growth loops are visible in the flow.** The App Store rating prompt at the emotional high before the price (ratings drive installs). A referral code field (creators with codes). The tracking prompt framed as "relevant content" (paid ads measured). Unrot has the tracking prompt and a web checkout but no rating ask and no referral.
- **Consistency.** Unrot says 1M+ users on the welcome, 290,000 in the chat, 200K on the plan page. Brainrot says 650K+ once.

## What to take, in order

Within our rules: their numbers only, round down, no body claims, no invented facts or counts, no tracking prompt, the paper stage, one idea a page, answers never auto-advance, a plain "we" voice.

1. **"What have you tried?" and the answer to it.** One question after the year page (or after the promise): *Leaving it in the locker · Screen Time limits · Just willpower · Nothing yet.* Then one page that answers their pick in our words: Screen Time limits have an Ignore button and GymBlock doesn't until you finish; the locker lasts until the water fountain; willpower is fine for set one. It closes the free alternatives before the price, which neither of our pages does today.
2. **"What does it cost you?"** One multi-select early, after "Be honest": *My workouts run long · I lose focus between sets · I skip the last sets · I get home late · Nothing, I just want it locked.* The promise and the paywall then echo the pick ("Home 35 minutes earlier." for "I get home late"). This is the user's own stated principle, from their answers, applied to words as well as numbers.
3. **The paywall as Brainrot's three beats.** It's a dashboard paywall, so this is a RevenueCat task: the shield on a phone as the first thing seen, the trial timeline with the reminder in writing (we already schedule it), and "No payment due now" next to the button. Consider the trial on yearly only; today's plans each show a price a month.
4. **Let the answer answer back.** The phone-minutes wheel is the moment for the Brainrot slider's trick. As the wheel runs past the rest they need, the stopwatch keeps running and the feed keeps scrolling: the thing they'd see at the gym, no graph, no mascot.
5. **Beyond the year, maybe.** Brainrot's 22 years is the loudest beat. Ours would be their minutes × their days × a stated horizon ("at 4 workouts a week for 10 years: 50 days at the gym on your phone"), rounded down, with the qualifier. Sulav's call: he cut the gym-time question because the per-workout number is what sells, and a decade number may dilute it.
6. **Ask for a rating, but after the first finished workout.** Brainrot asks before the price to lift the store rating. Ours would be honest after a workout with every rest on time, and likelier to be five stars.
7. **Real proof when it exists.** The App Store rating and count can go on the welcome once there are enough to show; never before.

## What not to copy

- **A mascot.** The brain is the biggest shared trait and a brand decision for Sulav, not a page change. GymBlock's mark is a kettlebell and the user has rejected figures. The honest substitute is a scene that reacts to the person's own answer, which V8 already does.
- **Fake loaders** ("Analyzing your answers…"), invented facts ("Did you know?"), counts and testimonials we don't have, university crests.
- **The tracking prompt.** GymBlock never shows it (TikTok SDK on SKAdNetwork only).
- **Snark.** "you're not special", "willpower → lol". The plain "we" voice stays.
- **Coloured stages, sunbursts, wastelands.** The paper stage stays; emerald is the only signal, red only for time on the phone.
- **A close button on the paywall**, and a weekly anchor used only to make yearly look cheap.
- **Coins and photo tasks.** Not our product.
