# GymBlock design

Onboarding follows **docs/ONBOARDING-V5-PLAN.md**. The app after onboarding follows **docs/WORKOUT-V6-PLAN.md** (V6 replaces the training and navigation parts of docs/UX-RESET-V3.md).

## Visual system

One look everywhere, set by onboarding: the *paper* stage (warm off-white, a faint peach light in the middle, quiet ink dots) behind every page, in light appearance. SF Pro with Dynamic Type; titles bold, labels medium, numbers monospaced. Liquid Glass on iOS 26 for every control: prominent white glass for the one primary action, glass steppers, rings, tiles, chooser rows and round icon buttons, with translucent fallbacks before iOS 26. Native Lists and Forms (settings sub-pages, editors, history details) sit on the same stage under the native iOS 26 Liquid Glass navigation bar; the Settings page (the Profile tab since 11 Oct 2026) follows SleepBlock's (10 Oct 2026): a 28-pt title (a round glass close button only when shown as a sheet), small uppercase section labels, and white glass cards of one-line rows (an emerald icon chip, a title, a quiet value, a chevron or switch), with Delete account as faint text below the account card; Home and the workout put their controls in that bar as toolbar items rather than drawing their own.

Ink (#141417) is the action; emerald (#0F9A6B) is the signal and nothing else — the live set, a finished rest, today in the week, a like-for-like gain, the "on" state of a toggle, the phone arc in the estimate. Picker values are grey. Never a coloured background. (The red ember and a graphite-and-mint scheme were both retired on 8 Oct 2026; paper was chosen from three rendered directions.)

Keep 24-point margins and a fixed grid. The primary action sits in exactly the same place on the workout and the summary (as on every onboarding page), and at the same size just above the tab bar on Home; a 20-point caption slot above it and a 44-point secondary slot below it are always reserved, so it never moves.

## Onboarding

Full spec: docs/ONBOARDING-V5-PLAN.md. Twenty pages (eighteen for Rarely), one question or idea each: welcome; name; gender; height; weight; phone between sets; minutes per rest; phone time against training time; hours a year as 45-minute workouts; paired story stages (mind-muscle, pump, progress); app blocking; rest alerts; hold to commit; offer; set up your splits (add, name, pick exercises; optional).

**Colour.** One *paper* stage on every page: warm off-white with a wide peach wash and a tighter core behind the stage area, ink dots that strengthen towards it (drawn once per screen size, `DotGrid`; every token lives in `JourneyTheme.paper`). No page uses a coloured background. Three surface tones: the stage, raised white glass (a faint top-lit rim on every card and control, `JourneyRim`), and the one ink action. Emerald (#0F9A6B) is the signal — ≤2% of a screen, always the one thing to look at: the phone arc, a finished rest, today, a gain, the check on a chosen answer. A chosen answer rises (brighter glass, ink edge) and is never a coloured block. Tracks, hairlines and quiet fills are ink at low opacity (`JourneyColor.ink(_:)`). Captions meet WCAG AA (≥ 4.5:1).

**Brand.** The mark is a cast-iron kettlebell with a keyhole: a wide trapezoid handle whose horns flare past the bell and curve into its shoulders, a round bell with a flat base, and the keyhole in the emerald signal, the only colour and the only lock cue (`BrandMark`, the same geometry as `scripts/generate-app-icon.swift`). It is ink on paper everywhere: the app icon (the mark at 78% on the plain paper field), the wordmark on the welcome page, and the badge on the account and offer cards. Chosen by the user on 9 Oct 2026 from rendered options and restored the same day after a dumbbell-and-lock detour (glass, flat emerald, real materials) that the user asked to roll back: "it's gotta be like before". **Red.** One warm colour, `JourneyColor.danger` (#DB4036), exists for time lost to the phone: the phone arc on the reveal ring and the dots on the hours-a-year page; training is the emerald signal. Nothing else is red. The launch screen is the stage colour and the first page rises into it: no white cover, no flash.

**Type and scale.** Headlines are `.title` bold with slightly tight tracking, centred in a two-line box so a one-line headline never sits above a hole. Section labels (`Eyebrow`) are the subheadline style in sentence case, secondary colour; hero numbers are `.largeTitle` bold; no tracked capitals, no fixed-size display fonts; numbers are monospaced. Everything is compact: 50-pt primary button, 52-pt answer rows, 18–20-pt card radii, 16-pt card padding, a 160–220-pt ring, 26-pt stepper digits, 44-pt hero numbers.

**Material.** Liquid Glass (iOS 26) for every control: prominent white glass for the primary action, glass answer cards, back button, notification cards, tiles and info cards; translucent fallbacks before iOS 26.

**Motion and touch.** Calm, one clock per page change: everything that changes fades out (0.18 s), the page swaps unseen, everything fades in (0.35 s); a stage shared by two pages stays put. Low-bounce springs; pages slide 18 pt in the direction of travel as they fade (Back slides back); scenes start after the page settles; Continue fades in when a scene ends; Home's cards settle in one after another on first appearance. Selecting an answer only selects; Continue moves on. Haptics are designed per moment: iOS-style double taps for notifications, crescendos for counting, a landing thud for reveals, rising impacts for each set, a ramp for the hold.

**Illustration.** The approved arm artwork (pre-rendered poses) and the brain-and-nerve scene carry the story pages. Blocked apps are the real icons and names iOS draws from the Screen Time choice, on glass tiles; empty tiles (the first with a plus) before anything is chosen.

**Voice.** Qualifiers stay, written as a person would say them: "Stays on this iPhone. Never shared.", "Illustrative, not a measurement.", "An example log, not your data.", "Uses Apple’s Screen Time. Pausing your workout lifts it.", "Subscriptions aren’t switched on in this build yet."

## Training and navigation

Two tabs, Home and Profile, in the native iOS 26 floating Liquid Glass tab bar (`MainTabs` in `Navigation.swift`; ink marks the chosen tab; a selection haptic on change). Added 11 Oct 2026 at the user's request. The workout and its summary fill the screen without the bar; a running workout always comes back first. Home's Start workout sits just above the bar, so it keeps the workout button's size but sits higher; from the first set to the summary's Done the button keeps one place. **Profile** is the Settings page as a tab: the 28-pt "Profile" title with no close button, a "You" section first, and a paper fade behind the status bar since the root has no navigation bar.

**Home** — what's next, from logged workouts only (12 Oct 2026). Top to bottom: the greeting as a 28-pt title ("Good morning, Sirish"; the name only when there is one); the week as seven small dots with the streak ("2 week streak", "Week 1" before the first workout); then progress straight on the paper, up to three exercises in turn (`ProgressCarousel`: the split that's up next first, then the biggest gains; a page every 4 s with a slide, a swipe holds the clock for 12 s, small dots beneath, still under Reduce Motion), each, no card and no gridlines — the exercise and what's held constant, the gain in emerald (a drop in grey, never red), an emerald line over the faintest fill through the last eight comparable workouts, only the latest a point, with its value just above it ("19 kg"). No dates, no scale, no gridlines, no split names. Behind the plot the page's own dots darken a little (`DotField`, on `DotGrid`'s lattice), easing in over three dots so there is no edge; Home's page dots run 1.25× darker than other pages. Only like-for-like workouts: same split, reps or load held constant. Before there is a line the same space says "Start a workout to see your progress" (or "Repeat a workout…" once there is history) over the dot field, nothing else, never sample numbers. Tapping a page opens that exercise's progress. Then, low on the page just above the button, "Up next ⌄", the split's name large and one line of what's ahead; "Up next" opens the chooser. "Sample data" sits in the caption slot above Start when demo history is loaded, otherwise the blocking caption. **Start workout** starts the queued split (or a free workout) in one tap.

**Workout** — top bar (the workout clock doubles as Pause/Resume, Sets, End); exercise name + chevron (opens the picker) with set dots ("Set 2 of 3", target = last time's set count, else 3); the ring (ready: last time's best; set: the set clock; rest: fills to the rest length, then red with "Rest's up" and a double haptic; the length is changed in place); weight and reps glass steppers (plate-grid steps, hold to repeat, tap to type; typing hides the ring so the button stays above the keyboard); "Last set ›" for one-tap correction; one honest caption; the primary button. Start set → Finish set → rest → Start set; after last time's number of sets it becomes `Next: <exercise>` with `Another set` beneath (free workout: Next exercise; split done: Finish workout). A 0.6 s guard stops double taps. Zero reps finishes as a missed attempt. Settings → Time each set off makes it one tap per set (timing marked unknown, never guessed).

**Interruptions** — everything is a saved timestamp, so locking, calls and relaunches lose nothing. Rest's up is a local notification when locked; the Live Activity (Lock Screen and Dynamic Island) shows the set or rest clock, the next set and a Start/Finish button that carries its intent so a lagging display can't double-log. A workout idle for an hour gets a "Still training?" reminder and, on return, an offer to finish it at its last set. Implausible set clocks (< 3 s, or > 15 min for a rep set) are kept but marked unknown. **Pause** freezes the workout clock and the running rest or set (the ring dims, "Paused" in red, Resume becomes the primary action, rest alerts are held and blocking lifts); resume moves the anchors forward, so paused time never counts as training, rest or set time.

**Summary** — an eyebrow (split · date) and a headline that changes with the workout ("Stronger than last time, Sirish." when there is a gain, "First one in the log" for the first workout, otherwise "Nice work"), one hero card counting up weight moved (or reps) with a soft glow, three small tiles (time, sets, average rest), "Better than last time" (same split, reps or load held constant, red arrows), a per-exercise recap, Done; Save as split for free workouts. Ending a workout confirms with an alert whose Keep going is always visible.

History opens on a glass card with the period total and a weekly bar chart (this week in emerald; Reps / Weight moved pills), then exercise progress (the scope defaults to the most recent workout's; Before/After tiles, the difference as a 44-pt headline, an inline line chart, the records) and the workout list with full exercise names; a workout can be deleted from its detail page.

**Split editor and exercise picker** — the editor is a native list on the stage: the name as a title-sized field, numbered exercise rows with their area, drag to reorder, swipe to remove, an Add row, and a footer that says so. The picker groups the catalogue by area with a check that fills in the signal colour, a running count on Done, search, and an "Add “…”" row when the search finds nothing. Editing exposes weight/reps first and timing/date/warm-up under Details; unknown timing stays unknown. Profile (settings) order: you (name, email when signed in, body), subscription (Gym Block Pro and its renewal, Manage subscription; only with an active plan), workout (rest length, rest alert, time each set), blocking (toggle and Apps to block, which opens Apple's picker), app (units, language, haptics, sound), your data (training answers, data), help (support, privacy, terms), account (sign in or log out; Delete account below), then the version.

## Data and boundaries

Preserve local history, exercise/split IDs, actual reps, load conventions, timing provenance and sample markers. Demo loading is explicit/idempotent and never replaces existing work. Blocking is Apple Screen Time and works on a real iPhone only; no live billing/account/backend service is configured. The Live Activity is local (no push).

Build success does not prove visual quality. Verify actual simulator layouts, full routes, keyboard and larger text, appearance, corrections, counter continuity and before/after records. Hardware haptic feel, physical-device handling and user comprehension need separate evidence.
